    About this map

    Personal tool, not an official forecast or warning service. For official watches and warnings use weather.gc.ca. For flight planning use NAV CANADA.

    Source rules

    1. Publicly accessible sources only. No restricted networks or logins.
    2. Every source has a published reuse licence.
    3. No DND/CAF METOC products.
    4. ECCC first. Other agencies fill gaps or serve for comparison.
    5. ECCC alerts are shown as ECCC publishes them, unaltered.
    6. No military sources (for example, no Joint Typhoon Warning Center).

    Sources and licences

    • Environment and Climate Change Canada (MSC GeoMet): models (GDPS, GDWPS, GIOPS, RAQDPS), wind animation and isobars (GDPS 10 m wind and sea-level pressure), radar, lightning, satellite, warnings. Contains information licenced under the Data Server End-use Licence of Environment and Climate Change Canada. No endorsement by ECCC is implied.
    • Open-Meteo: point forecasts from GEM (ECCC), ECMWF IFS and GFS (NOAA). Weather data by Open-Meteo.com, CC BY 4.0. Free tier, non-commercial use.
    • NOAA Aviation Weather Center: METAR and TAF, worldwide. US Government public data.
    • NASA GIBS: global satellite imagery, sea surface temperature, satellite rain estimate, Blue Marble relief.
    • OpenStreetMap: street base map. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
    • Natural Earth: city names and positions. Public domain.
    • ECCC weather alerts: read live from ECCC's open alert service (api.weather.gc.ca), text shown as published. Colour and type meanings quoted from canada.ca.
    • Natural Resources Canada, Canadian Wildland Fire Information System: Canadian fire areas (M3, estimated from satellite hotspots) and fire weather at hotspots. Open Government Licence – Canada.
    • NASA FIRMS: satellite fire detections worldwide, VIIRS 375 m (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21), last 24 hours. NASA open data; acknowledgement: NASA LANCE FIRMS.
    • NOAA National Hurricane Center: active storms, forecast cone, track and points. US Government public data. ECCC Canadian Hurricane Centre track and cone are used instead when it is tracking the storm.

    How it stays current

    A scheduled job runs every 30 minutes. It pulls METAR/TAF, fire detections, Canadian fire areas and tropical storms, looks up the current ECCC and NASA layer names and times, checks every feed, and republishes this site. ECCC alerts are read live by the page every 5 minutes. The Feeds button shows the last check.
